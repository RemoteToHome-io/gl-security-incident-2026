# Findings

One row per finding. "How it is known" uses four labels:

- **GL statement**: said by GL.iNet or its staff, with a link.
- **Verified**: checked first-hand by the named party, with the method.
- **Reported**: posted by someone else and not reproduced here.
- **Unconfirmed**: a single claim with no supporting evidence yet.

Times are UTC. Add rows through a pull request or an issue; see [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Not yet known

1. Whether any router was made to contact an address other than `170.39.226.219` and
   `66.245.219.252` (A-6). `65.20.71.131` is reported for KVMs; it is in none of our memory images.
2. Whether devices with the cloud service enabled but not bound to an account could be sent the
   command. Reports say the client keeps running and connecting when unbound (R-33). A test on a
   router of our own, unbound and with the old firmware, would settle whether binding matters.
3. Whether the fixed firmware of every model carries the same handler fix. We have read it in one
   (R-19). The clean-up is the same in the seventeen we unpacked (R-23); the GL-E5800 build is
   unread.
4. Whether the KVM implant was ever installed on a router. Its installer is written for routers
   too, and the attack searched 98 of our routers for its folders and found none (R-26); we know
   of no first-hand report of its files on a router (K-13).
5. How the older backdoor in R-11 was installed, and what rewrites the VPN server settings file
   at that moment.
6. How many devices were reached. Our own count is in the [field survey](field-survey.md); the
   community dashboard's is in G-12.
7. Whether the second stage of the older backdoor was ever given a task on any router. None was
   in memory on the 47 we captured (R-30).
8. Things we could still check on devices of our own: that the LuCI process page is empty on a
   Qualcomm model (G-13); the registration record of `gl-inet.top` (A-7); the two detection
   rules for the second stage under `yara` itself.
